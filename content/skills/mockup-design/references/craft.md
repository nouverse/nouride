# What a person sees that a script cannot

`render.mjs` measures whether a page can be read and pressed and whether it fits. It cannot tell a good screen from
a bad one. This is what to look for in the pictures, every round, and why. None of it says what a page should look
like: that comes from the person, from what is theirs, or from the request (SKILL.md, "This skill has no look of its
own"). It says how to tell whether the look you were given, or chose, was carried out.

## The first glance

- **One thing is seen first, and it is the thing the screen is for.** Look at the picture for a second and look
  away: what did you see? If it was the logo or a decoration, the thing that matters is too quiet. Size, weight,
  space around it and its place on the page are what make something first; a second loud thing beside it makes
  neither first.
- **Then a second, then the rest.** Three levels are enough for most screens: the point, what supports it, and
  everything else. A screen where everything is the same size has no order, and one where everything shouts has none
  either.
- **What is pressed looks pressable, and the main action is the plainest one.** One main action per screen. The
  others are quieter, and a dangerous one (delete, cancel) is never the loudest.

## The phone is its own layout

- **Read the phone's pictures as a person holding a phone.** The first window is all they see before they scroll:
  is the point in it? A desktop's wide header, three columns and a sidebar do not become a phone's screen by
  getting narrower.
- **One column, the important thing first**, the navigation in a shape a thumb reaches, things to press a
  fingertip apart from each other.
- **No sideways scrolling of the page.** A row of cards or a wide table may scroll sideways inside its own box,
  where the person can see it continues.
- **Text is read at the size it is drawn.** What was comfortable on a desktop may be a wall on a phone: fewer
  words, shorter lines, more space between blocks.

## Space and lines

- **Things that belong together are close; things that do not are apart.** Space is how a page says what goes with
  what. Even space everywhere says nothing.
- **Edges line up.** Follow a left edge down the page: the heading, the text, the form, the button. Each thing that
  sits a few pixels off is noticed as "something is wrong" without being found.
- **A few sizes of space, used again and again**, read as a system. A different gap everywhere reads as an accident.
- **Room to breathe at the edges.** Text and things to press do not touch the edge of the window or of their card.

## The same thing looks the same

- A button is one shape, one height and one treatment wherever it is; so is a card, a field, a heading of a given
  level. Compare them across the screens side by side: this is the thing nobody notices until the third screen.
- Colours mean one thing each. If one colour is "press here", nothing that cannot be pressed wears it.
- The same word for the same thing on every screen: "Order" does not become "Purchase" on the next one.

## The words

- **What the screen would really say**, in the person's language, the way their customers talk. Headings that say
  the point, buttons that say what happens ("Pay Rp120.000", not "Submit").
- Numbers, money and dates as their country writes them.
- **Try the long case**: the longest name, the largest amount, a title of two lines. A layout that only works with
  short sample words breaks on the first real one.
- Nothing wrapped badly: a button's label on two lines, one word alone on a heading's last line, a number broken
  from its unit.

## Theirs, not yours

- Put the mockup beside what is theirs (their site, their app, their logo) and look at both: the same colours, the
  same type, the same corners, the same voice? A mockup that could be for anyone is for no one.
- With nothing of theirs to match, the look still has to fit who it is for: a clinic's booking page and a game's
  landing page do not look alike. If your mockup would suit either, it suits neither.
- Not the look of the last thing you drew. A habit of yours is not their brand.

## What never to do

- **Never make text smaller to make it fit.** Say less, or give it room.
- **Never draw a device or a browser window inside the HTML.** The screen is the screen; `frame.mjs` puts it in a
  phone and a laptop when the person asked for that.
- **Never send what you did not look at**, at both widths.
- **Never leave a thing that looks pressable and is not**, or a link that goes nowhere, in a mockup sent as a link.
- **Never invent a fact about their product** to fill a screen: a price, a feature, a customer's words. Ask, or
  mark it plainly as a sample.
