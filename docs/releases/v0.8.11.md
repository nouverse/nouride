**0.8.11 updates from 0.8.0 or later.** From 0.7.x, start 0.8.0 once first:
[Upgrading to 0.8](https://nouride.com/en/docs/deployment/upgrading-to-0-8/) has the way across.

One database migration, `073`: a mission keeps its result beside its report. 0.8.11 keeps a snapshot of the database
before it migrates, named `…-before-073.db`. 0.8.10 refuses a database 0.8.11 has migrated and says so; to go back,
`nouride db restore` that snapshot first. The event protocol is unchanged, 1.6.

On Docker, check your configuration with the new image before you switch:
`docker run --rm -v ./nouride:/app/.nouride ghcr.io/nouverse/nouride:0.8.11 nouride config check`, with the volume your
compose file mounts.

### What a mission sends when it ends

- **Its result, as the message.** Two or three sentences saying what it achieved. The chat had its report cut to 1,500
  characters instead.
- **Its whole report, as `report.md`.** Every time a mission hands one back, in a repository or not: the full account of
  the work, as long as it needs to be. A result that waits for your yes comes with it, so you read it before you decide,
  and it is not sent again when you accept. A mission that fails or is stopped sends the report it wrote.
- **In your language, written formally,** as a document is, whatever way you chat.
- **The files made for you, not the code.** A document, an image, a spreadsheet goes as a file. Code a mission changed
  in a repository stays on its branch, which the notice names; it was sent as files.
- A `report.md` holding the value of one of the agent's credentials is not sent, and the chat is told where the report
  is: on the mission's page.
- **Read `report.md` in the dashboard.** On the mission's page, **report.md** opens the whole report rendered, over the
  page, with Save and **Save as PDF** — the report alone, printed, which the browser's print dialog saves as a PDF. A
  `.md` file in a dashboard chat opens the same way, where it was a download.
- **Each part of a mission's message on its own.** Started, a draft, a question, done: an empty line between the first
  line, the result, the branch, the link and what to send. They ran together, one line after another.

### Nothing you wrote is cut

- **A mission's goal** is whole when it starts and when an agent proposes one. It was cut to its first line, at most
  100 characters, when it started, and to 800 when proposed. The checks stay in `/mission show`.
- **What you tell a mission while it works** reaches its reviewer and its page whole. The reviewer read the first 500
  characters of each, and judged the work against part of what you said.

### A mission from a chat is a draft first

- **Asked for long work, an agent drafts a mission** and runs nothing until you start it. The draft says its goal, how
  many checks it has, who signs it off and whether it has a brief, with **Start it** and **Drop it** buttons; in a chat
  without buttons, the commands. It used to start at once, and nobody saw the checks or the brief before it ran.
- **Its brief is `BRIEF.md`** in the mission's folder, shown on the mission's page; each turn of the mission is pointed
  at it. On the page it is folded until you open it, and **BRIEF.md** opens it whole, as **report.md** does. A brief
  the goal names by its path is shown too, where it is in the agent's own folder.
- **Change a draft before it starts.** Tell the agent what to change and it sends the draft again saying what changed,
  or change it on the mission's page in the dashboard: goal, brief, checks, the reviewer, your accept, folder, branch,
  hours and turns. Once started, a mission's goal and checks stay as they are.
- **Start it however suits you:** the button, `/mission accept <id>`, the dashboard, or by telling the agent to go. It
  passes your words on, and they are kept in the audit log with the start. In a group, only the person who asked for
  it or an admin can start it, as with the button.
- **"Do it now, I'm off to sleep"** still starts at once. The *started* notice quotes your words, and they are kept in
  the audit log.
- **Who signs it off follows who is there.** A draft waits for you to start it, so it is yours to accept once its
  checks pass, unless the agent chose a reviewer; started at once while you are away, the checks and a reviewer. You
  can change that in the draft.
- **A draft nobody starts stays** under needs you, marked **Needs you · draft** on the dashboard, not "accept" as a
  result waiting for your yes is. Dropped, its folder goes with it.
- **When work is a mission is said by example**, no longer as "more than about ten minutes". Many files, many steps, a
  long research or a wait on CI is a mission. A hotfix, a small lookup or one change is done in the chat. An agent whose
  turn runs long is now also offered drafting a mission, beside handing the rest to a helper.
- **A branch only for code.** A mission works on a branch of its own only for code in an existing git repository.
  Agents are now told never to make a repository for a mission, and to work on a person's documents where they are.
  Asked to audit a spreadsheet, an agent had made one, and the mission worked on a branch of a copy.
- **A mission being checked no longer holds its agent's slot.** Its checks and reviewer run, and the agent's next mission
  can start meanwhile. `max_running` still counts it, and one folder still takes one mission at a time.
- **Missions at once, per agent.** One agent may have its own number, 1 to 4: on its Settings page, **Missions at
  once**, or `[missions] per_agent` in its own `config.toml`. Without one it follows the daemon's `[missions]
  per_agent`. The box's `max_running` still holds them all, and may be set as high as every agent at its own number.
- **`config.example.toml` had half a mission's limits.** It set `limit_turns = 50` and `limit_tokens = 50000000`; the
  defaults have been 100 turns and 100 million tokens since 0.8.0. A `config.toml` made from it keeps those two lines:
  take them out to follow the defaults, or set them under **Limits** on the Missions page.

### Agents answered by Claude Code or Codex can do more

- **In a group, they tag the other agents properly.** They are told each agent there with the exact text that tags it,
  such as the bot's id on Discord, what each one is for, and how many hand-offs a chain may run. A hand-off used to go
  through `ask_agent`, which nobody in the room saw. In a DM they are told that `ask_agent` reaches the others and a tag
  does not.
- **Nouride lends them more tools:** making a new agent, the skill tools, finding an MCP server to add, keeping a
  credential sent in chat (`secret_save`), and opening a file sent earlier in the conversation (`attachment_read`). As
  before, each one only where the agent's tool switches allow it, through the same approvals as any agent. A skill they
  make now goes into the household's pool like any agent's: on for them, and on the Skills tab for the other agents.
- **A file from earlier in the conversation**, from before a `/new` or one sent to another agent in the room, reaches
  them as a file they can open, a picture included.
- **A credential sent in chat** is no longer described to them as something their shell can use, because it cannot.
  Asked to keep it, they save it, and their CLI is started again with it at the next message. That happens only once
  nothing else of that agent is at work, so no other conversation is cut off.
- `nouride`, `update` and `session_status` stay with agents answered by their own model.

### Links to the dashboard

- **Ask an agent about a mission, a scheduled job or an approval, and it gives you the link to its page.** A mission's
  *started*, *needs you* and *done* notices carry one too. Agents are told to give the link their tool hands them and
  never to write one themselves.
- **Only with `[dashboard] public_url` set.** Links are built from it, so they open from a phone. Without it there are
  none, rather than a `localhost` address.
- **In a direct chat, only for someone with a dashboard account,** an id `web:<account>` in their People entry. Anyone
  else gets the same answer, report and files, without the link. In a group the link is written whoever is there; a
  person without an account who opens it sees the login page. The links `mcp_search` and a new coding-CLI agent's
  sign-in give follow the same rule; without one, the way there is said in words.
- **Signing in keeps the link.** One opened signed out goes to the login page and, once you sign in, on to the page it
  named. It used to land on Overview.
- **No preview card of the login page.** A message holding a dashboard link is sent with its preview off.
- **One job on Schedule.** `/schedule?job=<id>` opens the page with that job's card marked and its runs open.
- **A mission's checklist comes first on a phone,** before its turns. It came after every turn.
