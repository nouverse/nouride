Nouride Home is an Android app that runs Nouride on the phone itself. It carries a small Debian system
inside, runs the official installer in it on the first start, and keeps Nouride running in the
background. There is nothing to type in a terminal, and the phone does not need to be rooted. Other
devices on the same Wi-Fi open the dashboard from the phone.

It does what [An Android phone as a server](/en/docs/deployment/android-server/) does by hand with Termux:
the same Linux build of Nouride, the Router edition, with the [Nougate](/en/docs/providers/nougate/) LLM
gateway inside.

<Aside type="note" title="Tested on one phone">
A Redmi 12 (HyperOS on Android 15, 6 GB of RAM) with Nouride Home 0.1.4 and Nouride 0.8.22, on
2026-11-08: the first start, the dashboard on the phone and from a computer on the same Wi-Fi, an update
of the app from the version before, and a restart of the phone. Not tried yet: other phones, and a phone
where Android's limit on child processes has not been raised (see [Keep it running](#keep-it-running)).
</Aside>

## What you need

- **A 64-bit phone on Android 7 or later.** Nouride has no 32-bit ARM build, and Android refuses to install
  the app on a 32-bit phone.
- **About 700 MB of free storage.** The app, Debian and Nouride took that much on the test phone after the
  first start. What your agents download and build comes on top.
- **The internet for the first start**, which downloads Nouride, and **a charger**: the phone runs all
  the time, so keep it plugged in.
- **The same Wi-Fi** as the devices you will open the dashboard from, if you want to open it anywhere but
  on the phone.
- **A model account or an API key.** The Router edition signs in to an account you already have (Claude
  Pro or Max, ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot, Google Gemini or Kiro) through its built-in Nougate gateway; a
  provider's endpoint and API key work as well. The setup wizard asks.

## Install

<Steps>

1. ### Download the app

   On the phone, open [Nouride Home's latest release](https://github.com/nouverse/nouride-android-appliance/releases/latest)
   and tap `nouride-home-<version>.apk` under Assets. Android asks once whether your browser may install
   apps: allow it. Google Play Protect may offer to scan the app first, as it does for apps from outside
   Google Play. Nouride Home is not on Google Play.

2. ### Press Start

   Open Nouride Home and press **Start**. Android asks whether Nouride Home may run in the background:
   allow it, or Android may stop Nouride when the screen is off.

   The first start unpacks Debian and installs Nouride. It takes a few minutes, and the app shows each
   step. Keep the phone on Wi-Fi and plugged in until it says **Nouride is running**.

3. ### Open the dashboard

   Press **Open Nouride**. The dashboard opens already signed in, on **Personalize Your Account**: choose
   the username and password you will sign in with from now on, on the phone and on any other device. Then
   the setup wizard asks for a model and your first agent, as on every platform. Carry on at
   [First run](/en/docs/start/first-run/#open-the-dashboard).

</Steps>

## Open it on another device

The **Connect** tab has a QR code and the address, `http://<the phone's address>:18254`. On a tablet, a
computer or another phone on the same Wi-Fi, scan the code with its camera or type the address into its
browser, and sign in with the username and password you chose. **Copy** and **Share address** send the
address on.

**Open from other devices**, the switch at the top of the tab, is on as installed. Turn it off on a Wi-Fi
you do not trust: Nouride then answers only on the phone itself. Nouride starts again for a moment to
take the change.

Your router may give the phone another address later. Reserving one for it in the router's DHCP settings
keeps the link working.

## Keep it running

Nouride Home starts Nouride again if it stops on its own. What it cannot do alone is on the **More** tab,
under **Keep Nouride running**:

- **Battery.** The card says whether the phone lets Nouride run with the screen off, and **Allow** asks
  again if it does not. Until it is allowed, Home shows a reminder.
- **Xiaomi, Redmi and POCO.** Tap **Open settings**, turn on **Autostart**, and set **Battery saver** to
  **No restrictions**. Without Autostart, HyperOS does not let Nouride start again after the phone
  restarts.
- **Android 12 and later** stop an app's child processes when there are too many, and Nouride and every
  command an agent runs are child processes of Nouride Home. The test phone had that limit raised. If
  Nouride stops while agents are busy, raise it as in
  [the phantom process killer](/en/docs/deployment/android-server/#android-12-and-later-the-phantom-process-killer).

### After the phone restarts

Nouride starts again by itself once the phone has been unlocked for the first time after the restart:
Android starts no app before that. On Xiaomi, Redmi and POCO phones this needs Autostart, above. If you
turned Nouride off before the restart, it stays off.

## Updates

Nouride Home looks for a newer version by itself, and **Home** says when one is ready. **Check for
updates**, on **More**, looks at once. Press **Update**: the app downloads the new version and Android asks
you to install it. The first time, Android also asks you to allow installing apps from Nouride Home. Nouride,
Debian and your agents stay as they are, and Nouride starts again by itself.

## When something goes wrong

**Technical Logs**, at the bottom of **More**, has the details whoever helps you will ask for: what
Nouride Home did, Nouride's state, and its log.

## Removing it

Uninstall Nouride Home. That deletes Nouride and all of its data with it: agents, conversations, secrets,
the gateway's accounts.
