**0.8.0 upgrades from 0.7.28 or 0.7.29 only.** On anything older, update to 0.7.29 first and start it once — that
start brings every old form forward on disk — then take 0.8.0. On Docker: set `image: ghcr.io/nouverse/nouride:0.7`,
`docker compose up -d`, wait for it to say it is ready, then set the image back. 0.8.0 started on data 0.7.28 has not
run on refuses to start, before it writes anything, and says how across.

One database migration, `069`, and the event protocol is 1.5: `usage.purpose` may be `second_look`. At its first boot
0.8.0 rewrites each pack's `fallback_model` into `fallbacks` (below); nothing else about an install is rewritten.
Scheduled jobs from 0.7 keep running exactly as they did.

### Missions: long work an agent finishes alone

Give an agent a goal, walk away, and come back to work that is done — or to one clear question. A mission runs on its
own, turn after turn, through a restart, and is done only when it has been checked.

- **Nobody has to type a command.** An agent turns chat work into a mission when the work needs one — many steps or
  many items, longer than a reply, work that has to go on while you are away ("do it, I'm off to sleep") — and says
  so. Its notes and its plan go with it, and the chat keeps one line that becomes the outcome. `/mission <goal>` in a
  chat, the dashboard's **Missions** page and `POST /api/control/missions` are the explicit ways. Work nobody asked for
  — an agent's own idea after a job finished — waits for your yes: `/mission accept <id>`.
- **Its context stays small.** Every turn starts from the mission's page — its goal, its checks, its checklist and
  notes, its folder, the last failed check, the questions it asked — never from the turns before it. Hours of work
  are a page a turn, not one long conversation.
- **Done means checked.** When the agent says it is done, the daemon runs the mission's own check commands in its
  folder, as the agent's commands run (at `balanced`, as a scheduled job's do): one that fails is the next turn, with
  its output. Then a **reviewer** — a fresh context on the agent's high tier, holding only tools that read — checks the
  result against the goal, with the agent's report as the claim it is checking; its fail is the next turn too.
  `review = false` skips it. Where no command can tell — a summary, a draft — you accept it: `/mission accept <id>`.
  The same failure three times in a row stops and asks you.
- **The chat stays quiet.** A mission says it started (with how to stop it), that it needs you, that it is done (with
  its report, and its result files as files), or that it ended — and a progress line, written from its checklist, at
  most every half hour.
- **Watch it on the dashboard.** **Missions** lists what needs you, what is working and what finished, each with its
  checklist's progress — a tab for each, a search, and a filter by agent. A mission's page shows its steps, turns, hours and tokens against their limits, the step it is
  on as it runs, its checklist with each step's note, the calls parked for you (answered there), its questions, its
  report and files, and every turn — with Stop, Continue, Accept and Answer where they apply.
- **An approval does not stop it.** A call that needs you is parked: the prompt comes to your chat, the mission goes on
  with what does not depend on it, and your answer — any time within a day — is its next turn. It waits for you once a
  turn finishes no step: checking on the approval is not progress. A mission that ends withdraws what it parked.
- **It runs as you.** Your approval level and your People entry apply, and a prompt it raises is answered from your
  chat.
- **Limits, kept by the daemon:** 8 hours of work (time waiting for you is not counted), 100 turns, 100 million tokens.
  Reaching one stops its jobs and asks: `/mission continue <id>` gives it the same limits again. One mission runs at a
  time per agent, and on the box half the agents that are on, rounded down — at least one, at most eight, or as many
  as all of them if you set it. One repository is worked on by one mission at a time. These five are
  `[missions]` in `config.toml` — `per_agent`, `max_running`, `limit_hours`, `limit_turns`, `limit_tokens` — and
  editable on the dashboard's **Missions** page, with no restart, where an empty field is its default. A waiting
  mission holds no slot.
- **A restart does not lose it.** A running mission carries on from its page. A call the restart cut off is named: one
  that only read, it runs again if it needs to; one that may have changed something, it checks before redoing; one the
  approvals ask about — a delete, a send — it does not repeat, and waits for you. A mission that keeps dying across
  restarts with no step finished stops.
- **A place of its own:** a folder under its agent's private one, removed a week after it ends — or `workdir`, an
  existing folder or repository, which is never removed.
- **Its own conversation, not yours.** Resetting, deleting or starting a new chat leaves a mission running and
  reporting there. Its conversation is not in the Chat list; ended, the mission is kept as long as conversations are,
  `[retention] session_days`, and one still open is never removed.
- **Inside a mission, an agent can hand a separate piece to another agent** as a mission of its own — one level, no
  deeper — and is told how it ended.
- **When it ends**, it is filed once in its agent's memory, from its report. Its turns stay out of the nightly pass.
- **From a chat:** `/mission` lists what runs and waits here, needs-you first; `/mission stop|continue|accept <id>`,
  `/mission answer <id> <answer>`. **From a shell:** `nouride mission [list|show|stop|continue|accept|answer]`. An id is
  the short one a notice shows (`msn_3f2a91`).
- **The Missions page** lists them under tabs — Needs you, Working, Done, Failed, Stopped — each named by its goal's
  first sentence. A mission's page shows its checklist, its turns with the steps they took, its hours and tokens
  against their limits, and what it waits for.

### Long work on a schedule: missions

A scheduled job or a webhook can now run a **mission** each time it fires, the fourth kind beside a text, a script and
an agent turn. Each run survives a restart, parks what needs a person, and counts as done once its checks pass and the
reviewer agrees. Nobody accepts each run: whoever made the job accepted the work then. One run at a time; a run due
while the last still works is recorded as skipped, naming it. A webhook's body is on the run's page as data and marks
the run as having read outside content. The job's chat hears when a run is done, needs someone or ends, not that it
started on schedule.

The Schedule and Webhooks forms offer it (a goal, checks one a line, the reviewer's choice), the lists say
"Mission: …", and a run's result links to the mission it started. From a terminal:
`nouride cron add --mission <goal> [--check <cmd>] [--no-review]`. Reminders and scripts stay what they were: no model,
no cost.

### Scheduled work an agent proposes, and you turn on

Ask an agent for anything at a time or again and again ("every Monday at 9, sum up the customer mail for the team")
and it **proposes** the job with a new `schedule` tool. Before, it ran `nouride cron add …` and you approved a raw
command line. The job is written whole and kept off, and a card in the chat says what it does, when, and that results
come back to that chat:

```
📋 nouva proposes a scheduled job: Customer mail
When: cron `0 9 * * 1` (Asia/Jakarta)
Does: a mission for nouva: "Sum up last week's customer mail for the team." — then a reviewer agrees
Results: here
It stays off until somebody turns it on.          [Turn on] [Discard]
```

- **Turn on** starts it, and it runs as whoever pressed. **Discard** lets it go. In a one-to-one chat the person in it
  answers; in a group, the person who asked or an admin; never from another chat. Without buttons (WhatsApp), the card
  names `/schedule on <id>` and `/schedule discard <id>`.
- **The Schedule and Webhooks pages** list proposals with the same two buttons; a card answered there says so in the
  chat. A proposed webhook's URL and a proposal's Run appear once it is on.
- **An agent never turns a job on itself**, widens one or edits one that runs. A change is a new proposal naming the job
  it replaces; turned on, that job takes the new shape and keeps its id, its runs and its webhook URL. The `nouride` tool
  refuses an agent's `cron add|update|enable` and `webhook add|rotate`, and points at `schedule`. At most ten proposals
  of one agent wait at once, and a webhook that runs a command stays an admin's to make from the dashboard.
- Every kind goes this way: a reminder, a script (its command shown whole), one agent turn, a mission.

### Two guards on work nobody watches

A mail read every morning is where a prompt injection does its harm: one message says to forward every invoice
somewhere else, the agent may send, and nobody is there.

- **A scheduled job's run sends only where its job says.** A `send_message` to any other chat is asked first, at every
  approval level, `trusted` included: parked in a mission, refused on the spot in a turn that cannot wait.
- **The second look.** In work nobody watches (a scheduled or webhook run, a mission) that has read outside content, an
  action that sends something off the machine is weighed first by a model that sees the work's goal and the action,
  never the content. That covers a message to another chat, an MCP tool its server does not mark read-only, and a
  command that posts, uploads, copies to another machine, pushes or mails, or runs inline code that uses the network
  (`python3 -c "import urllib…"`). The model may add a question, never remove
  one, and the question says why. When it cannot be reached, a person is asked.

**Always for this job.** Either guard's question can be answered for good, for one job: the chat, MCP tool, host,
address or remote it named is allowed for that job's runs, and nothing else. A mission's question has an **Always for
this job** button; a scheduled run cannot wait, so what its guards held is kept on the run, and its history on the
Schedule or Webhooks page offers **Always allow for this job**. A job's card lists what it is allowed, each with **Take
back**. A command that does not say where it sends is asked every time.

`[security] model_guard` sets the second look: in the daemon's config, in a pack for one agent, and as a choice on an
agent's Access tab. Unset follows the level: on at `careful` and `balanced` on the agent's high tier, off at
`trusted`. A scheduled or webhook run is never `trusted`, so it looks; a mission goes by the level of whoever it runs
as. `off` turns it off, and a tier or a model id keeps it on at every level. A value that is none of these
refuses to start.

### Each agent its own user

On a Linux install with systemd, each agent's commands now run as that agent's own Linux user, `nr-<agent>`. One
agent's command cannot read another agent's private folder, the daemon's database or secret store, or a credential it
was not given — however the command is written. `install.sh` sets it up: a small root helper, `nouride-run`, and its
socket. `nouride doctor` says whether it is on and, if not, why.

- **Each agent has a HOME of its own**, its private folder. At its first start it gets a copy of the logins and
  settings the agents shared — `gh`, `.gitconfig`, a kube config, Claude Code's, `~/.ssh/config` — so what worked the
  day before works after. A login that renews its own token, as Claude Code's does, may ask one agent to sign in again.
- **What an agent installs is its own:** `npm i -g`, `pip install` and the rest land in its own `~/.local`. What was
  installed for every agent before — `claude`, Rust, global npm tools — stays on its PATH, to run.
- **The orchestrator installs and updates what every agent runs:** tell it, and it uses `nouride tools run`. On by
  default; the switch is on its agent page in the dashboard (Settings), or `[security] shared_tools = false` in its pack.
  No other agent can change a program another agent runs.
- **What `write` and `edit` save in an agent's folders is the agent's own file**, so its own command can make a
  script executable.
- **Job logs, attachments and long tool output move** from `.nouride/data/` to `.nouride/jobs`, `attachments` and
  `tool-output` at the first start, with every conversation that names them.
- **A pack can keep an agent out of the credentials directory:** `[security] credentials = false`.
- **`[security.exec] landlock`** — `auto` by default — adds Landlock where the kernel has it.

In Docker, on macOS, and on Alpine without the `shadow` package, commands run as the service user, as before.

### What asks: three presets, one list

How much an agent asks is one setting now, `[security] approvals` — or an agent's `[agent] approvals`, or your own
`/approvals` — over one fixed list of what always asks (`nouride policy` prints it).

- **`careful`** asks before each new command or change once, and *always* remembers it; everything on the list —
  deleting outside the workspace, tearing infrastructure down, force-pushing, stopping or replacing this daemon, its
  database, a job that runs unasked, an interactive session, and the rest — asks every time. A new install starts
  here: `nouride setup` and the dashboard's first-run wizard write it into `config.toml`.
- **`balanced`** asks only before a `config.toml` changes, someone is given access, this daemon stops, a stored secret
  is replaced or deleted, or a credential leaves this machine. An install that never set a level is `balanced`, as
  before — **and asks less than 0.7.x's `balanced` did**: a delete, a teardown, a service stop, a `sudo`, a credential
  file read now run without asking there. Where each agent is its own user, the operating system keeps it out of what
  is not its own whatever the level. Choose `careful` if you want those asked.
- **`trusted`** asks nothing, as before. The secret guard has no switch of its own any more: it asks at `careful` and
  `balanced`, and `trusted` answers it — `/secret guard` and `[security] secret_guard` are gone.
- **Under `careful`, a busy day says so.** After the fifth question in a day, the daemon adds one line under the
  question naming `balanced` and where to set it — once a day at most, and never once you have chosen a level yourself.

`[security.exec] mode`, `ask`, `ask_fallback`, `sensitive_paths` and `allowlist` went into these levels. A
`config.toml` that still writes one starts, with a warning naming it — except where leaving it out would let more
run without asking: `mode = "deny"`, `mode = "allowlist"`, `ask = "always"` and `sensitive_paths = "hardline"` are
refused at boot, each naming what to write instead. The dashboard's Sensitive paths card and Secret guard switch are
gone with them.

### Another provider and model when one is down

`[llm] fallbacks` is who answers when an agent's model cannot — a list, in order, each a provider **and** a model, since
a model's name means nothing on another provider:

```toml
[llm]
fallbacks = [
  { provider = "openrouter",   model = "anthropic/claude-sonnet-4" },
  { provider = "ollama-local", model = "qwen3:8b" },
]
```

- **After the provider's own retries,** the next pair answers. Another model on the same provider answers a rate limit,
  an overloaded provider or a model that is not served; another provider also answers one that is down, out of credit or
  refusing its key. A refused request is never retried on a fallback: any model would meet the same answer.
- **Each pair is tried once, in order,** and the rest of the turn stays on the one that answered. The chat's progress
  line says so, and the turn's usage is charged to that provider — its price, its billing.
- **The operator hears of an outage once,** not once a turn: the first fallback from a model in an hour, with the fix
  when it is theirs to make — a model not served, a key refused, a balance spent.
- **`[llm.defaults] fallbacks`** is the list for every agent whose pack names none. Every provider must be a configured
  profile, or the daemon refuses to start, naming the file and the pair.
- **The dashboard's Model tab** edits an agent's list: a provider and a model per row, added, removed, saved whole.
- **It replaces `fallback_model`.** At its first boot 0.8.0 rewrites each pack's `fallback_model = "x"` into
  `fallbacks = [{ provider = "<the pack's provider>", model = "x" }]`, the rest of the file as written. A pack that still
  says `fallback_model` is read as that one pair until the next minor, which reads `fallbacks` alone.

### Background work makes room for the machine

The nightly memory pass, its search indexing and the helpers an agent starts in the background now wait while the box
is busy, and go on when it is not. Busy is the CPUs more than 70% busy over the last minute, a thermal zone at a trip
point where the kernel acts on the heat (a fan's do not count), or the daemon's cgroup waiting for memory 10% or more of
the last minute. Each is read from the kernel's own files and counts only where they exist — a Mac is never busy. The
night waits two hours at most and a helper ten minutes (or half of `job_max_ms`), then they run anyway. The log, and a
helper's job log, say when the work waits, why, and when it goes on. Your chats, helpers an agent waits on while you
wait, and a pass you start with **Run now** or `nouride memory pass` never wait. There is nothing to configure.

### Spend warns before it stops

Past `cost_alert_threshold_pct` (80) of a daily cap, the operator was told — and told again every five minutes for the
rest of the day, since the alert rode the `[alerts]` cooldown. Now it is said **once at the threshold and once at 95%**,
each once a day and kept across a restart, and each names who spent it: the agents and models with the most of the
day's spend, in dollars, or in tokens on a token cap. The cap itself is unchanged.

### A model whose window is not known

A model that its pack, its gateway and Nouride's table of model families all say nothing about is budgeted at a 32k
window. Looked at first: an agent's prompt and tool schemas alone come to about 31k, so at that guess the history had
no room, and every tool result of a turn was cleared — the one the model had just asked for too, which it then asked for
again. The oldest results are cleared first now, and the results of the last round, which the model has not read yet,
are never cleared. Setting `[llm] context_window` in the pack, or a gateway that states the window in its model list,
still gives the turn its real room; the Model tab says when the window is a guess.

### Three model tiers, and a shorter config

- **`small_model` and `high_model` beside `model`**, in `[llm.defaults]` and in a pack's `[llm]`. Both optional; unset,
  each is `model`, so nothing changes until you write one. A pack without its own inherits the install's while it runs
  on the install's provider.
- **An agent picks a tier for the work it hands off:** `delegate` and `ask_agent` take `tier` — `small` for quick,
  simple work, `high` for hard work that has to be right, `default` when it says nothing. Its prompt names the three,
  what each is for and how they compare in cost, and which models they are. A helper runs on the asking agent's model
  of that tier; an agent asked answers on its own.
- **Every helper key takes a tier's name too:** `summary_model = "small"`, and the same for `[memory] pass_model`,
  `[media] image_model`, `[queue] steer_reply_model` and `[skills.learning] model` — each agent's own model of that
  tier, on its own provider. Helpers still run on each agent's own model until one is written; `pass_model = "small"`
  needs no `pass_provider`, and is refused beside one.
- **On the dashboard:** an agent's Model tab shows its three tiers, inheriting until it names its own; the Models page
  has the install's tiers and what each helper runs on, under **Model tiers**.
- **Thirty-six rarely-changed keys are marked advanced** — cooldowns and code lifetimes, the queue's and the
  scheduler's limits, `[llm.streaming]`, how the context window is trimmed, `[control]`, `credentials_dir`, the byte
  limits, mention hops. They work as before; they are gone from `config.example.toml`, listed once in the docs'
  Configuration page under Advanced settings, and the mention hop limit moved behind **Advanced** on Agent Settings.
- **Four are fixed values:** `[health]`, `[cron] tick_interval_ms`, `[queue] dedup_window_ms` and a gateway's
  `[resilience]` table. A file that still writes one starts, and each line is named in a warning — delete it.
- **A pack's `config.toml` warns about a key it does not read,** as the daemon's config always has: a typo like
  `[llm] modle`, or a table no release reads, used to load in silence and do nothing. The agent still loads.

### Whether a webhook can arrive

Each webhook now says whether a call from the internet can reach it: **local network only** (no public address, or one
on this network), **reachable** (a test through `[dashboard] public_url` arrived), or **unknown**, with why. **Send a
test** (`nouride webhook test <id>`) posts through the public address, marked as a test the route counts and does not
run. When it does not arrive, the card says what happened: the name does not resolve, the connection was refused, or
something else answered. It also says what to do: a box often cannot reach its own public address through port
forwarding, which a tunnel avoids; a name the box cannot look up most likely has no public DNS record, and the internet
cannot find it either. The manual has the rest.

Webhook deliveries are now counted, and a hook whose calls fail `fail_streak_limit` times in a row pauses itself, as
a scheduled job does. Until now the page read 0 deliveries and an empty Failing filter however many came. The card's
old **Test**, which ran the job by hand, is **Run now**. The address shown is the public one when it is set.

### The morning line

`[[people]] morning = "07:00"` gives a person one message a day, at that time, in their own chat, about what happened
while they were away: scheduled runs that failed, approvals and missions waiting on them, and missions that finished or
ended. It covers their own chats, jobs and missions; an admin hears about all of it, and what was spent. It is off for
everyone until switched on per person on the People page. It goes where they pick beside the time — one of their
Telegram, Discord or WhatsApp accounts, `morning_to` — or, unpicked, to the account they last wrote from. The daemon
writes it from its own records, so it costs no tokens. **A quiet morning sends nothing.**

### Asleep replaces Pause

`/sleep` puts an agent to sleep, `/wake` (or `/start`) wakes it — and `nouride agent sleep|wake <id>`, and **Sleep** and
**Wake** on its page. Asleep, it takes no work from anywhere: a message to it is not answered, and the chat is told
once an hour; a schedule or webhook due is recorded as skipped; `ask_agent` to it is refused; its missions wait. A turn
it was in stops at its next step, never mid-call. Jobs it started keep running, and what they report reaches it when it
wakes. A restart keeps it asleep. `/stop` still stops what it is doing at once and puts it to sleep. An agent putting another
to sleep or waking it through `nouride` asks a person, as giving someone access does. On Discord, the bot's own presence follows its agent: online
while it works, idle, do-not-disturb asleep. `nouride agent pause|resume` still work.

### Chats by name

Where a job's or a webhook's results go is picked as a chat, not typed as an id: the Schedule and Webhooks forms offer
each chat on the connection as what it is — **DM · Gading**, **Group · Nouride** — with its id beneath, and the cards
say it the same way. An id the daemon does not know can still be typed.

### The plan beside the chat

The dashboard chat shows the agent's plan above the box you type in while it works: steps done of all, the one in
hand, and the whole list a press away. Missions started from the conversation sit beside it with their progress or what
they need from you. A plan rewritten mid-turn shows at once, not when the turn ends.

### A turn a restart cut off

A chat's turn taken up after a restart now names the call it was cut off in, and treats it by what it was: one that
only read runs again if needed; one that may have changed something is checked first; one the approvals ask about is
not taken up by itself — the chat is told to check and say continue.

### What 0.8.0 no longer reads

0.7.22 to 0.7.28 rewrote every older form on disk at boot; 0.8.0 keeps none of those rewrites. A form that comes back
after that — a pack pulled from git, a hand edit, a script — is refused where it is read, naming what to write instead:
in `config.toml` the daemon does not start, in a pack that agent does not load and the others do, on the command line
the command stops with exit 2. The whole table is in `docs/DEPLOY.md` § The upgrade window.

- **`config.toml`:** `[security] sender_allowlist`, `[session_memory]`, `[daemon] default_agent`, `[[people]] id`,
  `[rsi]`, a `[[people]]` account written with the connection it came in on (`telegram:nouva:814` is `telegram:814`)
  or as `local:…` or `web:dev`, the approval presets' old names (`normal`, `risky`, `none`), Telegram `mode = "webhook"`,
  `bridge_transport = "stdio"` and `bridge_command`.
- **A pack:** the list form of `exec_secrets`, `memory_recent` or `task_plan` in a tool list, `[[mcp_servers]]` (a server
  is the library's, `agents/mcp.toml`), an old `[agent] approvals` name.
- **The CLI:** `--isolated` (write `--session isolated`), `--token-stdin`, `--key-stdin` and `--value-stdin` (write
  `--stdin`), `nouride approvals approve` (write `allow-once`) and `nouride people forget` (write `remove`). A script or
  cron job that still uses one stops with a message naming the word to use. The control API is the same:
  `POST /approvals/<id>/approve` answers 400, naming `allow-once`, `allow-always` and `deny`.
- **Read differently:** `Role:` in a pack's `IDENTITY.md` is no longer its headline (`Headline:` is); `USER.md` is
  neither read nor backed up; `/auto` in a chat is a message to the agent like any other — `/approvals` is the command;
  a `nouride chat` from 0.7.10 or older finds no `/activity` on a 0.8.0 daemon.
- **Data from before 0.7.28:** a database below the schema 0.7.28 leaves (68), or a memory vault still in 0.6's layout
  (day pages flat in `days/`), is refused with "install 0.7.28 first" and left as it is. `nouride db restore` no longer
  puts back a snapshot from before 0.7.28 — 0.7.28's `db restore` does, and starting 0.7.28 once brings it forward. The
  schema itself is one file now, `068_baseline.sql`, folded from the migrations 0.7.28 had; a database 0.7.28 left is
  taken as it is. 0.8.0 adds one migration, `069`: it drops `memory_cursors` (a chat's cut point is read from the
  memory vault now) and adds what missions, scheduled work and webhooks keep, so going back to 0.7.x is `nouride db restore` of the snapshot 0.8.0 took before it —
  `data/backups/db/nouride-<time>-before-069.db` (DEPLOY.md § Going back a release) — not a plain reinstall.
- **`nouride memory backfill` is gone.** To import 0.5 or 0.6 capture transcripts, run it on 0.7.28 before you update;
  nothing on disk is deleted.
- **WhatsApp:** a contact's session file from before 0.7.22 is no longer converted. It is read as absent, and the
  session is renegotiated on that contact's next message; the link to your phone stays.
- **`nouride doctor`** no longer has a "Ready for 0.8.0" section: what it listed is refused where it is read now.

### Smaller

- **`nouride jobs` shows what runs, then the last five that ended**, each command on one line, and how many older ones
  it left out; `nouride jobs --all` lists every job kept. An agent asked what was running read twenty finished jobs and
  their whole commands — about 10,000 tokens to learn that nothing was. `nouride jobs log <id>` still prints a command
  whole.
- **A new conversation starts from its agent's measured token count.** A chat's first request was sized by character
  count alone — a new dashboard chat was said to carry 31,391 tokens where the model counted 48,950 — and its window
  was decided at that number. It now starts from what the agent's last request measured on the same model, so the
  turn details show the request's size from the first one.
- **A Discord bot no longer stays at "connecting" after a slow login.** When a login timed out, the retry waited for the
  old connection to close, and it never did. The bot read messages and could not answer them until the daemon was
  restarted. That wait now ends after 30 seconds, on every chat platform, and an old Discord connection that opens
  late is closed.
- **`nouride doctor` no longer warns about a Python package the system ships**, such as Debian's `debconf`, which
  `pip check` calls missing although Python imports it.
- A turn that makes the same call and gets the same answer five times in a row ends there, told at the third that the
  answer will not change — an approval, a job's end and a person's reply each arrive after the turn, as a message of
  their own.

- `plan` takes a `note` on each step: what a finished step did and left. A mission's checklist runs to 30 steps.
- A mission's turns run on an internal connection, `web:mission`, which `/health` and the dashboard's connections do
  not list.
- **The People page on a phone** shows each person as a card of their own; an account id had a column a few letters

### Fixed

- **A message to a person's Discord account never arrived**: Discord answered Unknown Channel, since a user id names no
  channel, and a morning line went on to their next account. The person's DM is opened by their account now — for the
  morning line, and for `send_message` to somebody who has not written to that bot on Discord yet.
- **A scheduled run's refused call reached the agent as "A human denied this call"**, though nobody had been asked, so
  it could tell the chat that someone said no. It now says nobody was there to ask.

### Before you upgrade

- **A declared root** (`[workspace] roots`) outside the install is now reached as the agent's own user. Run
  `nouride doctor` after the upgrade: it names a root an agent cannot write, with the one command that grants it.
- **Rename a pack whose folder is not a user name** — capitals, underscores, longer than 29 characters — or its
  commands are refused on a Linux install.
- **From 0.7.28 or 0.7.29 only**, as above.
- **The checksum files are `CHECKSUMS` and `CHECKSUMS-<edition>`** from this release (`SHA256SUMS…` before it), signed as
  before. An updater older than 0.7.21 does not see 0.8.0 and stays on the newest 0.7.x, so it crosses the floor
  first; a script that checks a download by the old name needs the new one.
- **Check the configuration with the new build first:** `nouride config check` with 0.8.0's binary, or on Docker
  `docker run --rm -v ./nouride:/app/.nouride ghcr.io/nouverse/nouride:0.8.0 nouride config check`, with the volume your
  container mounts — pointed at an empty folder it says the defaults would start. On data no 0.7.28
  has run on, it says to start 0.7.28 once first, as the daemon would. Otherwise it names the old forms
  `config.toml` still says, and each agent that would not load, with why — and, as warnings that stop nothing, the
  fixed keys a copy of the old example still carries (`[health]`, `tick_interval_ms`, `dedup_window_ms`) and any pack
  key nothing reads.
